# Licensing FAQ

Plain answers to the questions the [Sarv Community License](../LICENSE) raises.
This page is a guide, not the licence — where they differ, the LICENSE file wins.

## The one-paragraph version

Use Sarv Inbox for anything you like, including running your business on it, for
free and with no user limit. Modify it as much as you want. Share it with others
free of charge, keeping our branding on it and publishing the changes you made.
What needs our written permission is making money from *the software itself* —
selling it, hosting it for other people, or shipping it inside your product — and
taking our name off it.

## Can I…

**…use it at my company, for commercial work?**
Yes. Any size, any number of employees, no fee, no permission needed. That is
[§4(a)](../LICENSE).

**…self-host it for my own organisation?**
Yes. §4(c).

**…modify it for my own use?**
Yes, freely. You only owe the community your changes once you hand the software
to someone else. §4(d), §7.

**…fork it on GitHub?**
Yes. A public fork is a distribution, so three things apply: keep it free, keep
the Sarv branding, and label it clearly as an unofficial modified build. §6.

**…give a build to a friend, a client, or the public, for free?**
Yes, if you meet §6: free of charge, branding intact, a visible "unofficial
modified build" notice if you changed anything, the LICENSE and NOTICE files
included, and your modifications published.

**…sell it, or charge for a build?**
Not without our written consent. §5(a).

**…run it as a hosted service for my customers?**
Not without our written consent. §5(b). This is the clause that keeps someone
from taking the work and reselling it as their own SaaS.

**…bundle it with the product or service my company sells?**
Not without our written consent — even if you give the Sarv Inbox part away
free. §5(c).

**…rebrand it, or ship it white-label?**
Not without our written consent. §5(d). Branding must stay exactly where you
found it: product name, icons, About screen, window title, installer metadata,
copyright notices, links back to us. See [TRADEMARKS.md](../TRADEMARKS.md).

**…use it as an internal tool that my customers happen to email?**
Yes. Your staff using it to answer customer mail is Internal Use. Giving your
customers accounts on it is not.

**…charge my consulting clients for setting it up?**
Yes. Selling your own time is not selling the software. What you may not do is
charge for the software itself, or fold it into a product you sell.

**…use it in academic research, teaching, or a security audit?**
Yes. §4(e). We would like to hear what you find — see
[SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md) for vulnerabilities.

**…keep my modifications private?**
Yes, as long as they stay private. §7 is triggered by distribution, not by
editing. Ship it to anyone and you have 30 days to publish the source of your
changes.

**…relicense my contribution, or my fork, under MIT?**
No. Contributions come in under this licence (see
[CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md)), and a fork is a Modification, so §7
requires it to stay under this licence too.

## Why not MIT or AGPL?

MIT would let anyone take Sarv Inbox, rebrand it and sell it as a hosted
service, contributing nothing back. That is the specific outcome this licence
exists to prevent.

AGPL would force the share-back but would still permit resale and rebranding,
and its network clause is harder for ordinary users to reason about than "keep
it free, keep the branding, publish your changes".

So this licence is deliberately closer to the
[fair-code](https://faircode.io) model that n8n and others use: maximum freedom
for users, a boundary around commercial exploitation. It is **not** OSI-approved
open source, and we do not describe it as open source. Calling it that would be
inaccurate, and inaccuracy is how projects lose community trust.

## What about the dependencies?

They keep their own licences — mostly MIT and Apache-2.0, with `ical.js` under
MPL-2.0. Our licence does not and cannot change theirs. See
[THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

## What if I get it wrong?

Fix it. A first breach that you cure within 30 days of noticing reinstates your
licence automatically (§13). We would much rather send an email than a lawyer.

## I want to do one of the things that needs consent

Good — that is a conversation, not a refusal. Write to
[licensing@sarv.com](mailto:licensing@sarv.com) and say what you want to build.
Commercial, OEM, hosting and white-label licences exist precisely for these
cases, and they can grant branding rights this licence withholds.

For trademark-only questions: [legal@sarv.com](mailto:legal@sarv.com).
